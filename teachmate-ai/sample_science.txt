Photosynthesis - Grade 8 Science Notes

Chapter 5: How Plants Make Food

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants manufacture their own food using sunlight energy. It is one of the most important biological processes on Earth.

RAW MATERIALS NEEDED:
- Carbon dioxide (CO2) absorbed from the air through tiny pores called stomata
- Water (H2O) absorbed from the soil through the roots
- Sunlight energy captured by the green pigment called chlorophyll

WHERE IT HAPPENS:
Photosynthesis takes place in the chloroplasts found in the cells of green leaves. Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, which gives plants their green color and is responsible for absorbing light energy.

THE CHEMICAL PROCESS:
6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy --> C6H12O6 (glucose) + 6O2

In simple terms: Carbon dioxide plus water, in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, produces glucose and oxygen.

PRODUCTS OF PHOTOSYNTHESIS:
1. Glucose - stored as food (starch) and used for growth and energy
2. Oxygen - released into the atmosphere as a by-product

TWO MAIN STAGES:
1. Light Reactions (in the thylakoid membranes)
   - Sunlight splits water molecules
   - Oxygen is released
   - Energy is stored as ATP

2. Dark Reactions / Calvin Cycle (in the stroma)
   - CO2 is fixed into glucose using the stored ATP
   - Does not directly require light

FACTORS AFFECTING THE RATE OF PHOTOSYNTHESIS:
- Light intensity: More light = faster rate (up to a saturation point)
- Carbon dioxide concentration: More CO2 = faster rate
- Temperature: Optimal range is 25-35 degrees Celsius
- Water availability: Lack of water slows photosynthesis

IMPORTANCE OF PHOTOSYNTHESIS:
- Provides food for plants and all organisms that eat plants
- Produces oxygen that all living things need to breathe
- Removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere
- Forms the base of almost all food chains on Earth

KEY VOCABULARY:
- Chlorophyll: Green pigment that absorbs light
- Stomata: Tiny pores on leaves for gas exchange
- Chloroplast: Organelle where photosynthesis occurs
- Autotroph: An organism that makes its own food
- Heterotroph: An organism that cannot make its own food
